1. Before you touch a new video
• NEW Order of work, every session: (1) read every new doc comment in every week, starting from Week 1,
  and reply to each one; (2) do the revisions; (3) only then start new videos.
• NEW Keep this handbook next to you for every video. If a recent note seems to contradict it, tell Joel rather
  than choosing yourself.
• NEW Work through videos in chronological order. There is no Trello; the doc is the system.
• NEW Minimum 5 new scripts per day, even while revising old ones. Build a routine; finishing 20 of 35 in a
  week is not acceptable. But don't rush volume either: stacked speed means stacked mistakes.
• NEW Know the channel. Turn on post notifications for youtube.com/@thelxthalfc and watch every video
  that goes out. Study 5–7 videos closely to learn how Joel talks about moments and which details in a
  description let him say certain lines.
• NEW Communication first. Ask questions before the work instead of saying “got it” and delivering
  surprises. Joel would rather talk it through than discover errors after.


2. First decision: which type of video is it?
Get this wrong and the whole video is a redo. Decide before you search for a single clip.

Type A — Clip / moment videos
Top 5 goals, moments, celebrations, skills, fails, disrespect, sportsmanship, etc. Anything where the clip
itself is the content.
• Ordered by the Viral Formula (Section 3). Never chronological.
• Sourced from viral reference videos (Section 4).
• You are the engine behind every pick. AI is not.
Type B — Facts / stats / list videos
Fastest goals, best save from every World Cup, Ballon d'Or if Messi and Ronaldo never existed, records, best
player born each month, combined XIs, kings of each league.
• NEW Quick test: if the title has a number or unit behind it (seconds for fastest goals, goals for most goals,
  years/editions), it is fact-based.
• Ordered chronologically or numerically, never by the viral formula. Counting starts at 5: lowest number →
  highest, or as the notes say (e.g. “Best save from every World Cup” runs 2026 – 2022 – 2018 – 2014).
  Always include the most recent edition — there is no excuse for a World Cup video with no 2026.
• NEW Lists must follow reality. World Cups are every 4 years, so parts run through real editions (e.g.
  2026–2010), never overlapping ranges like 2022–2018 then 2018–2026.
• These are “Claude jobs”: use Claude to build the list, then verify every entry yourself. You still write the
  explanation in your own words.
• NEW If the notes say “use Claude”, it is an instruction, not an option.
• The viral formula is not needed here, but the video still needs a strong start — say in the notes if you think
  an order change would help and let Joel decide.

Name / story lists (no number in the title)
NEW Players who retired too early, greatest player in every country's history, scariest defenders. Off-pitch,
player-focused content.
• NEW Search the exact title on YouTube first and check Shorts. Rotate keywords when results dry up
  (“players who retired too early”, etc.).
• NEW Shortlist only players who appear in viral videos on the topic, and verify each one fits the title (elite
  AND retired — if you can't confirm they were elite, cut them).

3. The Viral Formula (Type A, non-negotiable)
• #5 — Proven hook. The start clip of the most viral reference video. Never the least viral option.
  Attention is won or lost here; you never win it back later.
• #4 — Most engaging clip. Backed by comment data (quote the top comment and its likes).
• #3 — Engaging, less than #4, more than #2.
• #2 — Least engaging of the five. Still a banger — just the weakest banger.
• #1 — The infamous #1. Visually the best / represents the video style. Player-name weight matters here
  (~5%).
• NEW #1 is not “the best clip”. It's a notable #1 that is still good enough to feel like a #1. You hook viewers
  in inverse order: 4 down to 2 gets progressively less engaging. Check whether the reference used it at #1.
• Proven hooks outweigh comments. Order of evidence: (1) clip used as a hook in a viral reference, (2) a
  viral video on that clip, (3) comments. Comments only count for what they actually say — “Molina's shot
  was good” is not engagement; a controversial or reaction-heavy comment is. Most comment-based
  evidence belongs at #4, not #5.
• NEW “Most mentioned in comments” is not the same as “most used as a hook”. The hook always wins #5.
• NEW If every viral reference opens with the same clip, it is proven — never swap it (Bamford opened every
  chest-and-volley reference; Neymar opened both references).
• NEW #5 is a proven hook most of the time. The only exception is when you're deliberately improving on it
  — and then you say why in the explanation.
• NEW No proven hook exists? Use the moment with the most viral reposts / videos about it (e.g. Mbappé
  being ignored) and state that in the explanation.
• NEW Never reorder because commenters say “this should be first”. If we copy what worked, we get the
  same comments and the same engagement on our video. Replicate what people engage with — don't
  remove it.
• Decide slots while shortlisting (“this is clearly a #4”). Don't order at the end.
• Space out bait / wrong picks — never two next to each other. Two-thirds of the video must be obviously
  right so the bait reads as debate, not clickbait.
• Never Messi and Ronaldo in the same video at the start — nobody debates that.
• When the notes give a joke pick (e.g. “Antony at 1”), it is there for a reason. Do it.
• Do not invent your own formula. “Generally good at #1” logic is banned. Making your own version of the
  criteria has caused two rounds of errors already.

  A non-viral reference proves its clips are NOT engaging enough.

5. Clip Sourcing SOP (highlight links)
Workflow: build the video as usual (find clips, add references, explain every pick) → submit for review → once
approved, source the highlight link for every selected clip and paste it into that version.

Step 1 — Moment-focus or player-focus?
• Moment-focus — the script describes a specific thing on the pitch (goal, skill, incident). Find and paste
  the exact highlight link for each moment.
• Player-focus — the video is about a player generally (e.g. “Top 5 Richest Footballers”). Just add the
  player's name; the editor finds clips to match the audio. No links needed.
• NEW Even for player-focus videos, still search the moments on YouTube; you can briefly describe moments
  that have gone viral.

Step 2 — Finding the clip
• Search format: [Player] vs [Team] + the moment — “Roberto Carlos vs France free kick”, “Zlatan vs
  England bicycle kick”, “Messi vs Getafe solo goal”. Adding the year helps. Check the kits in the reference
  to confirm the match/era.
• Turn on a UK VPN first (any free one). UK-restricted channels like Sky Sports / Sky Sports Retro upload the
  best-quality, upscaled versions of iconic moments — usually the clip to use.

Step 3 — Picking the best version
• Open every decent candidate in its own tab and eliminate: 1080p minimum, least grainy; no watermarks,
  logos over footage, effects or filters; official uploads (broadcaster/club channels) over re-uploads; view
  count = credibility; full official highlight reels beat random re-uploads.
• Don't overthink it — if a clip is clearly acceptable, use it. But take an extra minute if you suspect a cleaner
  version exists.

Step 4 — Struggling? / Step 5 — Last resort

6. Read the title. Then read it again.
More redos come from misreading the title than from anything else. Define the key word before you start:
• Tournament = World Cup, Champions League, Club World Cup, Euros, Copa América — not a league.
• Leagues = one per country, consistent category. Don't mix Europe, a country, and a cup in one list.
• Legends = past professional footballers. Not randoms, not current players.
• Primes = player + era (“Messi 2010–12”) with a clip from that era. Not their best on-pitch moments.
• Disrespectful goals = shooting from absurd range, skilling defenders up, humiliation. Not just good goals.
• Worst World Cup winners = the weakest teams that won. Not each winner's worst World Cup.
• Best goalkeepers ≠ World Cup goalkeepers. Teams ≠ players. Free kicks ≠ penalties. Viral trend
  celebrations ≠ any celebration.
• NEW Words that sound related are not the same: “World” ≠ “World Cup”.
• NEW Retired = retired from football. Retired from international football is not retired (Neuer).
• NEW Fails = moments that genuinely make the player look bad. An ordinary miss doesn't qualify.
• There is one king of each league / competition. Never two.
• Watch the full reference video before picking so you understand its theme and why the creator chose each
  clip.
• If you understand 90%, ask so you understand 110%. Paste the title + notes into Claude and ask it to
  explain what's being asked, or ask on WhatsApp. Guessing is not an option.


7. The Notes field
• The notes are instructions, not suggestions. Read them before anything else. Ballon d'Or video =
  2008–2023 only. “May be parts” = make the parts. “Add Drogba at 5” = Drogba at 5.
• NEW “Add Carlos or Bale” style notes: if unsure whether it means one or both, ask — don't guess.
• If you want to go off-note, message Joel on WhatsApp first and wait for a reply.
• Never add things that weren't asked for (“ADDITIONS”, extra players, extra sections, suggestions inside
  descriptions). It creates confusion and slows everything down.
• If you change something across multiple videos that wasn't the norm, say so up front.
• NEW Notes are universal. Joel won't make the same note 20 times: once you see a note on one video, add
  it to your notes doc and fix it on every video.
• When a correction has been given once, check your other pending videos for the same mistake before Joel
  finds it. Repeating a corrected mistake across weeks is what turns a note into a redo.


8. AI use
• NEW “I hired you, not Claude. If Claude could do it all I wouldn't hire you.” Claude is a tool; you verify it
  works and every decision is yours.
• You decide. AI assists. For Type A videos AI never picks or orders clips. For Type B it can build the list —
  you verify every line.
• Verify AI output against reality: a combined XI runs GK – LB – CB – CB – RB – CDM – CM – CM – LW – RW –
  ST. Neuer is still playing. Yamal was not at the 2018 World Cup. A club cannot play a nation.
• For XI videos add who missed out for each position (e.g. “De Bruyne over X”).
• NEW AI descriptions are fine for off-pitch, factual content (career stories, retirements): paste the shortlist
  into Claude with “give a brief description of [player] + title”. AI descriptions are a no-go for on-pitch clips
  — anything describing what happens in a clip is written by you, second-by-second.
• NEW AI for grammar is fine only if the result still reads the way you wrote it. If it turns your line into flowery
  AI prose (“immense physical force… flattened like a pancake”), that's a rewrite, not a grammar fix.
• NEW Claude can't open links — describe or paste what it needs.

9. Descriptions
Format: Player vs Team. That is how Joel reads it out.
• Name the subject of the video in every clip. A Mbappé clip that never says “Mbappé” is a failed clip.
• Name every player involved where possible (Haaland AND Szoboszlai). Use the jersey-reading tool, search
  the moment on YouTube, or ask Aldrich. “Player in a blue kit”, “unnamed opponent”, “vs opponent”,
  “career stats unavailable” — all banned. Find it.
• Unknown player → describe by position or one feature (“the goalkeeper”, “female player”). Unknown team
  → leave it out. No kit-colour fashion descriptions.
• A club never plays a nation. If your description says Tottenham vs Colombia, something is wrong.
• Describe the clip, not the reference video. Describe what happened, not what the creator joked about.
• The description must serve the title. “Messi carried Argentina” means describe how he carried, not a full
  move breakdown. “Celebrations” means describe the celebration, not the goal. Don't spend lines on a
  player who isn't the subject.
• Second-by-second, as if for a blind person: where did the shot go, did the keeper save it, did it go wide.
  Then stop — no tangents.
• NEW Narrate it like a commentator would narrate each second of play. Say where on the pitch the player is
  and which part of the goal it went in (top left, bottom right). “Kicks the ball and scores” is poor.
• NEW Describe exactly what you see — wrong body parts or actions (Savinho “stomach” when it was a shot)
  go straight into the voiceover.
• NEW Write in the tone Joel reads out on the channel. Stiff, over-built sentences leave him no room for
  creative freedom and sound unnatural read aloud.
• Don't miss the beat that makes the clip: Zirkzee waited for it to roll in; the woman didn't know she'd
  scored; Ronaldinho went back and forth then back-heeled the nutmeg. These make or break the script.
• Do not describe or mock famous viral dances. Name it, move on.
• Add year / season when clips are similar (all Messi vs Real Madrid, Barça vs Real Madrid goals) so there's a
  differentiator.
• Add requested stats inside the entry (13/100 free kicks, sprint speed, record figures, finals lost, curses).
  Specify left or right when it matters.
• Simple mass-audience English. “Enforcers” → “defenders”. “Plant foot gives way” → “misses the throw”.
  You gain nothing from fancy words; we are trying to get millions of views.
• Only say “signature” / “iconic” / “famous” when certain. Every fact correct — never invent a player,
  match, or score (the fake Demba Ba entry; Bale's bicycle kick was vs Barça, not Liverpool). Comedic
  factual bending is Joel's call, not yours.
• Sarcasm and Joel's voice (aura, rage-bait lines, running gags like Ronaldo's “100,000 career goals”,
  “Ronaldo could never”) when it comes naturally, and always when the notes ask for it (e.g. “call the dive
  genius”).


10. Football terminology you must know
These mistakes have each caused a wrong description. The full terms list is in Appendix B — use the main
terms until they feel natural. NEW Before using any term you're not sure about, search it and understand it;
they don't all mean the same thing.
• Nutmeg — rolling the ball between an opponent's legs and getting past. Not “accidentally kicked it
  through his own legs”, not “possession turned over”.
• Taking a touch — controlling the ball. A first-time shot has no touch.
• Corner kick — the set piece from the corner arc. A goal scored from near the corner flag in open play is
  not a corner.
• Outside the box ≠ outside the pitch. The box is the penalty area.
• Intercept = stop the ball. Not dribble past.

LxthalFC Scriptwriter Handbook v3 — 10 Sept 2026                                                           Page 5
• Dummy / let it run — letting a pass go past you on purpose.
• Throw-in vs throwing the ball at a player.
• “Kick” is banned unless literally kicking a person. Say shot, pass, chip, cross, volley. Avoid “of
  PSG”-style phrasing — just say the team.
• “Attempts” ≠ did it. “Powerful dribble” means nothing. Spell football terms correctly (cleats, back heel) —
  the AI reads misspellings wrongly.


11. Explanations (every video, every clip)
• One line per number, a full phrase, never one word. “Viral”, “least”, “mostly mentioned”, “good hook” are
  not explanations. Put each number on its own line so it's easy to read.
• Data-backed: “5 — start clip of [reference link], 1.2M views.” / “4 — top comment ‘Neymar was so cool’,
  8,372 likes.” / “3 — more reacted than 2 in comments (pasted below).”
• NEW Say what actually proves it. If #5 is there because of comments, say comments — “good hook” tells
  Joel nothing (the Lukaku case).
• NEW Explain why the clip IS in the video, not only why you didn't use something else. Both together is best
  for context.
• For Type B: state the ordering rule used (“chronological, newest first”).
• Explain why THIS clip over the others, not why the whole video went viral.
• Your reasoning, not the AI's. If you used AI, say so in the explanation.
• Paste the comments you relied on — it helps.
• No essays, no feelings, no struggles — that goes on WhatsApp.
• Never skip. Writing the explanation is how you catch your own duplicate clips and wrong picks.

12. Process
• Colour code: Green = approved. Orange = fix. Red = remove/redo that clip. Red title = redo the whole
  video. Two+ reds in one video = full redo.
• NEW Status lives on the video number: its colour plus the approve time Joel adds. Approved means nothing
  left to do on that video.
• “Redo from scratch”: copy old work elsewhere, delete, restart the full flow — 15–20 clips → shortlist →
  order → describe → explain. You may paste previously-approved descriptions back in; nothing else from
  the old version.
• Template exactly. Separate parts as 19.1, 19.2 etc. so each can be approved, audio-linked, and edited
  independently. Fill every field. Never leave a section blank — “nothing here so I can't review” is a lost day.
• NEW Never mark comments as resolved. Reply under each one — “noted”, “done”, or your answer. Even a
  thumbs up. No reply means Joel can't close it and can't tell you've read it.
• NEW If you don't understand a comment, reply “I don't understand” — not “I think I changed this”. Joel
  never highlights something for no reason.
• Say “done” only when it is done, in the place Joel asked. Re-read the comment before replying — “done”
  on a video still in the wrong order gets re-opened.
• NEW When Joel asks “what do you mean?”, explain it a different, clearer way. Don't repeat the same
  sentence.
• NEW Scripts that already have voiceover: acknowledge the notes, don't edit them. Recheck every script
  that hasn't been read out yet.
• NEW Recheck means every word, and every word is intentional — not a quick gloss. Take two days if
  needed. For videos Joel hasn't reviewed, don't assume they're wrong; verify they're right.
• Check your own work before submitting: duplicates, spelling, title match, notes followed, order direction
  (we start at 5).
• Notes doc is mandatory. Every rule, every mistake, and every voice-note instruction gets written down as
  you listen — not remembered. Joel will not repeat himself.
• Ask questions when unsure. Re-ask rather than guess.
• NEW Repeated errors that waste review time may lead to penalties.

14. Recurring failures, ranked by cost
• Wrong video type: viral formula on a facts video, or chronology on a clip video.
• Title misread (goalkeepers vs World Cup, teams vs players, primes vs moments, worst winners, free kicks
  vs penalties).
• Notes ignored — including “use Claude” treated as optional.
• NEW Own criteria invented: “#1 = the best”, “most mentioned = hook”.
• Comments weighed over proven hooks.
• AI picked / explained and it wasn't verified.
• Wrong facts: wrong match, wrong team, club vs nation, player not present that year.
• Subject player not named; “unnamed / unknown” filler.
• Terminology errors (nutmeg, touch, corner, box).
• Missing the key beat of the clip.
• Explanations skipped or one-word.
• Non-viral or off-topic sourcing.
• NEW Doc comments not replied to / marked resolved.
• Same corrected mistake repeated in pending videos.

15. The big picture
The system is repeatable: read notes → pick the type → viral reference (A) or verified list (B) → best 5 →
formula or chronological order → second-by-second player-vs-team descriptions → line-by-line data-backed
explanations → checklist → submit → highlight links once approved. Once it's habit it runs on autopilot and
review takes minutes. Master the baseline, then rage-bait upgrades and the view-bonus pay structure kick
in.
